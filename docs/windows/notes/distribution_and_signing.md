## 1. Microsoft Store: registration cost, identity verification, package types, signing, review time, revenue share, own payment system

### Takeaway
As of the Learn page dated 2026-10-29, Store registration is free for both Individual and Company accounts when started from storedeveloper.microsoft.com; individuals verify with a government ID plus selfie and a personal Microsoft account. An MSIX submission is re-signed and hosted by Microsoft for free (no certificate needed), certification takes "up to three business days", and a non-game PC app may use its own payment system and keep 100% (or use Microsoft commerce at 15%).

### Cited Findings
**Fee and onboarding**
- [Official, 2025-09-10] Windows Developer Blog: "individual developers can now publish apps to the Microsoft Store without paying any onboarding fees"; "Developers will no longer need a credit card to get started"; the experience is "now globally available in nearly 200 markets worldwide"; ID + selfie verification is "supported in all markets worldwide". No excluded countries are named. — [Windows Developer Blog](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2025/09/10/free-developer-registration-for-individual-developers-on-microsoft-store/)
- [Official, page date 2026-10-29] "With the new onboarding experience, there are **no registration fees** for either account type, so you can create your developer account and start publishing at no cost." - [Official, 2026-10-29] The free flow has one entry point: "You must begin your journey at https://storedeveloper.microsoft.com. This is the only supported entry point for the new flow. Other paths (e.g. direct via Partner Center, Xbox, or Visual Studio) will show the legacy flow." — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/partner-center/open-a-developer-account)

- [Official, §10.8.4] "If your product restricts access to user-created content during or after a trial, you must notify users in advance." — [Microsoft Store Policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)

### Inferences
- MSIX through the Store is the only route that needs no certificate purchase at all: Microsoft re-signs, hosts, and updates. An EXE/MSI Store listing still needs the developer's own CA-chained Authenticode signature and own hosting, so it does not remove the signing problem.
- A free tier plus paid upgrade can be sold either through Store IAP (15% fee) or through the developer's own payment system (0% to Microsoft) under §10.8.1 / §10.8.6, provided the §10.8.2 conditions (identify provider, confirmation, PCI DSS, declare in Partner Center) are met.
- Account-type risk: the Learn page and §10.14 tie the Individual account to activity "not in relation to their business, trade, or profession". A solo developer selling a paid upgrade is arguably acting in a trade. Microsoft's text says Individual is "usually appropriate for a single developer working on their own", so this is ambiguous rather than prohibited; because Individual-to-Company conversion is unsupported, the choice should be made deliberately before the first submission.
- §10.8.3 wording includes "API secret keys": an app whose *primary* functionality required users to enter API keys would need a Company account. Optional AI-provider keys in a widget tool are probably not "primary functionality", but this is a reading, not a Microsoft statement.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the App Developer Agreement itself, so the 15% / 12% / 100% figures rest on a Learn marketing page last updated 2024-07-23 and the September 2025 blog, not the current contract text. The older "85% / 95%" structure from 2019 appeared in search results as superseded; exact current agreement version and date were not confirmed.
- Whether a paid one-time "unlock" that the developer sells on his own website (licence key) counts as a permitted "secure third-party purchase API" flow, and how the §10.8.2 donation sentence interacts with §10.8.1 for non-game PC apps, could not be resolved from the policy text alone.
- No official list of the "nearly 200 markets" was retrieved; the old "Account types, locations, and fees" URL now redirects to the open-a-developer-account page, which contains no country list.
- Certification time for EXE/MSI submissions specifically was not fetched (the three-business-day figure is from the MSIX page).

## 2. Microsoft Store Policies: version, and the sections that bear on an app that runs user-written or downloaded scripts, shows widgets, runs in the background, hosts shared content, or generates code with AI

### Takeaway
The current document is **Microsoft Store Policies version 7.20, published 15 September 2026, effective 22 October 2026**. There is no rule banning interpreters or script execution; the controlling rule is §10.2.2, which forbids using dynamically included code to change or extend the product beyond its *described* functionality or to introduce policy-violating features. A widget gallery shared between users triggers §11.12 (UGC), and live AI generation triggers §11.16 (disclosure and reporting).

### Cited Findings
All quotations in this section are from [Microsoft Store Policies 7.20](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies) [Official], fetched 2026-11-16; header reads "Document version: 7.20 / Publish date: September 15, 2026 / Effective date: October 22, 2026".

- **§10.2.2 (dynamic code)** — full text: "Your product must not attempt to fundamentally change or extend its described functionality or introduce features or functionality that are in violation of Store Policies through any form of dynamic inclusion of code. Your product should not, for example, download a remote script and subsequently execute that script in a manner that is not consistent with the described functionality" — [Store Policies §10.2.2](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.2 (security preamble)**: "Your product must not jeopardize or compromise user security, or the security or functionality of the device, system or related systems. You are solely responsible for all product safety testing, certificate acquisition (unless provided by Microsoft Store signing), and the implementation of any appropriate feature safeguards." — [Store Policies §10.2](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.2.3 (malware / secondary software)**: "Your product must not contain or enable malware as defined by the Microsoft criteria for Unwanted and Malicious Software. Further, your product must not offer to install secondary software that is not developed by you and does not enhance the functionality of your product." — [Store Policies §10.2.3](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.2.4 (dependencies)**: "Your product may depend on non-integrated software (such as another product, module, or service) to deliver its primary functionality if you disclose the dependency at the beginning of the description in metadata." Also: "Generally, dependency on non-Microsoft provided drivers or NT services is not allowed". — [Store Policies §10.2.4](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.2.7 (uninstall)**: "Your product must clearly communicate and enable a user's ability to cleanly uninstall and remove your product from their device." — [Store Policies §10.2.7](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.2.8 (changing the Windows experience)**: "You are required to use supported methods and must obtain user consent to change any user's Windows settings, preferences, settings UI, or modify the user's Windows experience in any way. Unsupported methods include but are not limited to use of accessibility APIs or undocumented or unsupported APIs in unsupported ways." — [Store Policies §10.2.8](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.1 (accurate representation)**: "Your product and its associated metadata ... must accurately and clearly reflect the source, functionality, and features of your product." — [Store Policies §10.1](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.3 (testable)**: "The product must be testable." §10.3.2: "If your product requires access to a server, the server must be functional to verify that it's working correctly." — [Store Policies §10.3](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.4.2 (startup/responsiveness)**: "Products must start up promptly, continue to run and remain responsive to user input. Products must shut down gracefully and not close unexpectedly. The product must handle exceptions raised by any of the managed or native system APIs and remain responsive to user input after the exception is handled." — [Store Policies §10.4.2](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.6 (capabilities)**: "The capabilities you declare must legitimately relate to the functions of your product, and the use of those declarations must comply with our product capability declarations. You must not circumvent operating system checks for capability usage." — [Store Policies §10.6](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§10.9 (notifications)**: "Your product must respect system settings for notifications and remain functional when they are disabled." §10.9.4: notifications "must relate to the product or to other products you publish in the Store catalog". — [Store Policies §10.9](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **Content scope (Content Policies preamble)**: "Content means the product name, publisher name, product icon, product description, the images, sounds, videos and text contained in the product, the tiles, notifications, error messages or ads exposed through your product, and anything that's delivered from a server or that the product connects to." — [Store Policies §11 preamble](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§11.11.3**: "If your product provides content (such as user-generated, retail or other web-based content) that might be appropriate for a higher age rating than its assigned rating, you must enable users to opt in to receiving such content by using a content filter or by signing in with a pre-existing account." — [Store Policies §11.11.3](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§11.12 (User Generated Content)**: "User Generated Content (UGC) is content that users contribute to a product and which can be viewed or accessed by other users in an online state. If your product contains UGC, you must: Publish and make available to users a product terms of service, code of conduct, and/or content guidelines for User Generated Content that are accessible from within the product and on your website. Provide a means for users to report inappropriate, illegal, or harmful UGC within the product to the developer for review and removal or disablement ... and/or implement a method for proactive detection of inappropriate or harmful UGC. ... Remove or disable UGC when requested by Microsoft." Where a third-party mod/UGC platform is integrated: "clearly disclose to users when content is not created, published, or sourced by the developer or publisher, including through an in-product disclaimer, dialog, or visual indicator where appropriate." — [Store Policies §11.12](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§11.13 (third-party digital storefronts)**: "If your product is a storefront, or enables access to a storefront, the storefront must: Publish and make available a developer and/or publisher terms of service and content guidelines for products listed in your marketplace. Provide a means for users to report inappropriate content ... Implement a method for review and detection of content that is in violation of your terms or guidelines and take enforcement actions." — [Store Policies §11.13](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- **§11.16 (Live Generative AI Content)**: "Products that contain dynamic content created by generative AI models in response to user inputs must: Disclose the use of live generative AI in the metadata. Note the use of live generative AI in Partner Center during the submission process. Ensure that dynamic content created by generative AI models complies with all applicable Store Policies. Provide a means for users to report inappropriate content to the developer. You must take appropriate actions based on those reported concerns." — [Store Policies §11.16](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- A full-text search of version 7.20 for "interpreter", "widget", "startup task" and "background" (in the sense of background execution) found no dedicated rule: the only hits for "script" are in §10.2.2, and there is no section addressing AI-generated *code* specifically (only §11.16 on live generative AI content). — [Microsoft Store Policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)

### Inferences
- §10.2.2 is conditional on the *described* functionality. An app whose Store description states that it runs user-authored and gallery-downloaded JavaScript/JSX widgets inside a sandboxed interpreter is executing scripts "consistent with the described functionality". The risk is not the interpreter but (a) a listing that under-describes it, or (b) scripts that add capabilities violating other policies (e.g. malware under §10.2.3, undeclared data collection). The declared-permission model (declared network domains, no native code loading) is a direct mitigating fact to put in the listing and in "Notes for certification".
- Because "content" includes "anything that's delivered from a server or that the product connects to", a curated in-app Gallery is Store-policy content the developer answers for. If users can publish widgets to other users, §11.12 applies (terms, reporting, takedown); a first-party curated, hash-verified gallery is closer to developer content than UGC, but in-app reporting is cheap insurance. §11.13 would only become relevant if widgets were *sold* in an in-app marketplace.
- An AI "generate a widget" feature is "dynamic content created by generative AI models in response to user inputs" in the plain sense of §11.16, so the listing metadata and the Partner Center submission must disclose it and the app needs a report mechanism. The policy has no code-specific AI rule.
- Run-at-startup, tray/background presence and desktop overlay widgets are not individually regulated in 7.20; the governing texts are §10.2.8 (supported methods + user consent for modifying the Windows experience), §10.2.7 (clean uninstall) and §10.6 (capabilities must relate to function). A packaged app's `StartupTask` is user-toggleable in Settings, which fits "supported methods".

### Gaps
- The change-history page (which sections changed between 7.19 and 7.20) was not fetched, so I cannot say whether §10.2.2 or §11.16 wording is new in 7.20.
- No Microsoft statement, certification report or precedent specifically about embedded JavaScript engines (QuickJS/V8) in Store apps was found; the conclusion that such apps are allowed is an inference from §10.2.2's wording and from the existence of scripting apps in the Store, not from an official clarification.
- The separate "product capability declarations" document (restricted capabilities such as `runFullTrust`, `unvirtualizedResources`, and whether they need justification at submission) was not fetched.

## 3. Windows Widgets Board: is package identity / MSIX required, and can an unpackaged app obtain identity?

### Takeaway
Yes: a Widgets Board provider must be a **packaged** Win32 app (or a PWA); the provider is registered through MSIX manifest extensions. An unpackaged app can obtain package identity via "packaging with external location" (sparse package) on Windows 10 2004+ — but the identity package must be signed with a certificate trusted on the user's machine, and Microsoft's sparse-package documentation does not list widgets among the features it unlocks.

### Cited Findings
- [Official, page dated 2026-10-23] "Currently you can implement a widget provider using a packaged Win32 desktop app or a Progressive Web App (PWA)." and "For a packaged desktop app, you implement a widget provider that responds to requests from the widget host and sends updates through WidgetManager." — [Widget providers, Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/widgets/widget-providers)
- [Official, same page] Capability/version table: lifecycle + Adaptive Card updates need Windows App SDK 1.2; user customization 1.4; web content / resource requests / messaging 1.6.3 ("also check the web-widget host and regional requirements"); analytics callbacks from "Windows 11 build 26900". "The widget host can activate and release your provider independently of the lifetime of pinned widgets." — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/widgets/widget-providers)
- [Official, same page] Widget content model: "You can provide an Adaptive Card template and data, or use a web widget to display HTML from a URL. Web widgets still use an Adaptive Card JSON payload to specify the content URL." — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/widgets/widget-providers)
- [Official, page dated 2026-10-23] The C++/WinRT walkthrough: "This walkthrough registers the widget provider through an MSIX package manifest." Registration uses a `com:Extension` ComServer ("the packaged app equivalent of registering a COM server by setting a registry key") plus `<uap3:Extension Category="windows.appExtension">` with `<uap3:AppExtension Name="com.microsoft.windows.widgets" ...>`. Prerequisites: "A Windows 11 device with the Widgets Board available for testing" and developer mode enabled. The page text contains no mention of unpackaged apps, sparse packages or external location. — [Implement a widget provider in a win32 app (C++/WinRT)](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/widgets/implement-widget-provider-win32)
- [Official, page dated 2026-10-03] Sparse packaging: "Granting your app a package identity (also called a *sparse package* or *packaging with external location*) unlocks Windows platform features that are otherwise unavailable to unpackaged apps: toast and push notifications, background tasks, app extensions, share targets, file associations, startup tasks, privacy consent prompts, and Windows AI Foundry APIs — all without switching to full MSIX packaging or changing your existing installer." — [Grant package identity by packaging with external location](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/desktop/modernize/grant-identity-to-nonpackaged-apps)
- [Official, same page] OS floor: "The `AllowExternalContent` feature used here was introduced in Windows build 10.0.19041.0." The manifest declares `runFullTrust` and `unvirtualizedResources`, `uap10:TrustLevel="mediumIL"`, `uap10:RuntimeBehavior="win32App"`. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/desktop/modernize/grant-identity-to-nonpackaged-apps)
- [Official, same page] Signing: "In order to be installed on end user computers, the identity package must be signed with a certificate that is trusted on the target computer. You can create a new self-signed certificate for development purposes ... but a production certificate from an IT Department or a service like Azure Trusted Signing will be required to register the package on end user computers." `Publisher` "must match the `Subject` of the certificate used to sign the application". A self-signed package fails with `CERT_E_UNTRUSTEDROOT (0x800B0109)` unless the certificate is imported into Trusted People. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/desktop/modernize/grant-identity-to-nonpackaged-apps)
- [Official, same page] Registration from an installer: `Add-AppxPackage -Path <PackagePath> -ExternalLocation <ExternalLocation>` (per-user) or `PackageManager.AddPackageByUriAsync` with `AddPackageOptions.ExternalLocationUri`. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/desktop/modernize/grant-identity-to-nonpackaged-apps)

### Inferences
- Package identity is a hard requirement for Widgets Board integration, and it cannot be had for free outside the Store: both a full MSIX and a sparse identity package must be signed by a certificate the target machine trusts. Inside the Store, Microsoft's re-signing satisfies this at no cost. So "Widgets Board provider + individual developer without a CA certificate" effectively means "MSIX via Microsoft Store".
- The sparse-package list includes "app extensions", and the widget provider registration is an `windows.appExtension`; it is therefore plausible that a sparse-packaged app can register a widget provider, but Microsoft's widget docs only describe the full-MSIX path.
- The Widgets Board renders Adaptive Cards (or a web widget pointing at a URL), not arbitrary native drawing. A JSX-to-native widget runtime would have to translate its element tree to Adaptive Cards for the Board, or else draw its own desktop windows (which needs no package identity at all).

### Gaps
- Not confirmed: an explicit Microsoft statement that a widget provider works (or does not work) from a package with external location. Needs a prototype or the WindowsAppSDK issue tracker.
- "Regional requirements" for web widgets (the docs refer to them; e.g. whether web widgets are limited to certain regions) were not fetched.
- Whether the Widgets Board is available/enabled on Windows 11 devices in China and whether third-party widgets appear there was not researched.

## 4. Code signing outside the Store: Artifact Signing (ex-Trusted Signing), OV/EV certificates, SmartScreen, Smart App Control

### Takeaway
Azure Trusted Signing was renamed **Artifact Signing** (GA reported January 2026) at US$9.99/month, but Public Trust certificates for **individual developers are limited to the United States and Canada**; organizations in China are also not on the eligible list. For a China-based individual the only public-trust options outside the Store are a traditional OV/IV certificate (about US$219+/year, hardware/cloud HSM mandatory, max 460 days validity) — and since 2024 neither OV nor EV gives instant SmartScreen reputation. Unsigned binaries get the "Windows protected your PC" prompt and are blocked outright by Smart App Control unless they already have positive reputation.

### Cited Findings
**Artifact Signing**
- [Official, page updated 2026-10-08] "Public Trust certificates are available to organizations in the United States, Canada, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Switzerland, Norway, and Israel. **OV / EV certificates**
- [Official, page updated 2026-08-17] "EV certificates no longer bypass SmartScreen. - [Official, same page] Behaviour table: Microsoft Store — "No warning — covered by Microsoft's certificate"; Valid Certificate (OV/EV) — "Warning — app flagged as unrecognized until reputation accumulates; verified publisher name is displayed"; No signature — "Warning — 'Windows protected your PC'; User must choose 'Run anyway' before the app can run. - [Official, same page] Reputation mechanics: two signals (publisher reputation, file-hash reputation). - [Secondary, CA statement] DigiCert dates the EV change: "In March 2024, Microsoft changed the way MS SmartScreen interacts with EV Code Signing certificates"; Microsoft no longer guarantees EV-signed apps avoid warnings. — [DigiCert Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.digicert.com/alerts/ev-signed-application-showing-microsoft-defender-smartscreen-warnings)
- [Secondary, reseller listings 2026] Entry-level OV code signing (Sectigo/Comodo; "Individual Validation" for solo developers) is listed at about US$219/year (US$219.45/yr in one 2026 guide). — [SSL Dragon](https://www.ssldragon.com/ssl-certificates/code-signing/), [SSLInsights Sectigo guide 2026](https://sslinsights.com/sectigo-code-signing-certificate-guide/)

**Unsigned apps / Smart App Control**
- [Official, page updated 2026-08-17] "On Windows 11 devices, the Smart App Control feature may supersede SmartScreen Application Reputation. Smart App Control will block execution of unsigned files unless the file has a positive reputation. Smart App Control signature checks apply to all executable files, not just those downloaded from the Internet." — [SmartScreen reputation, Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/smartscreen-reputation)
- [Official, same page] "Do not modify signed files"; "Do not sign potentially unwanted applications ... or the certificate may develop **negative** reputation"; "Use a consistent signing identity — changing your signing certificate affects the publisher trust signal". — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/smartscreen-reputation)

### Inferences
- For a developer who is an individual resident in China, Artifact Signing Public Trust is unavailable on both routes: individuals must be in the US or Canada, and China is not in the organization country list. This is the single biggest constraint on non-Store distribution.
- Because SmartScreen "Mark of the Web" checks apply to browser downloads, a package-manager install (winget/Scoop) of the same unsigned binary avoids the SmartScreen download prompt but not Smart App Control, whose "signature checks apply to all executable files".
- With annual (460-day) certificates, key continuity across renewals matters: publisher reputation follows the "same publisher identity", so a renewal that changes the subject name could reset reputation (a Q&A thread reports a reset after EV renewal — [Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5900208/smartscreen-reputation-reset-following-ev-certific); anecdotal).
- Cost ranking for a China-based individual in November 2026: Store MSIX (US$0, no warnings) < traditional OV/IV certificate on cloud HSM (roughly US$219+/year, warnings until reputation builds) < EV (more expensive, no SmartScreen benefit).

### Gaps
- Exact 2026 EV certificate prices and current OV prices from each major CA (DigiCert, Sectigo, GlobalSign, SSL.com, Certum) were not individually verified; the US$219/year figure is a reseller floor, and cloud-HSM/eSigner signing plans often add a monthly fee that I did not price.
- Whether a mainland-China individual can pass a CA's Individual Validation (accepted ID documents, notarization) and which CAs sell IV certificates there (Certum is commonly cited for open-source developers) was not researched.
- Whether Artifact Signing accepts a Hong Kong/Singapore/other-jurisdiction company owned by a Chinese resident is a legal-entity question I did not research; Singapore organizations are on the eligible list per the quickstart.
- The Azure pricing page did not render dollar figures; US$9.99 is confirmed by the Learn SmartScreen page, while the US$99.99 Premium and US$0.005 overage figures come from a search summary of the pricing page.
- Share of Windows 11 consumer devices with Smart App Control actually enabled (it can be on only for clean installs / evaluation mode) was not researched.

## 5. Other channels (winget, Scoop, Chocolatey, GitHub Releases, Steam) and auto-update options

### Takeaway
winget accepts a manifest PR pointing at the publisher's own HTTPS installer; the documented requirements are silent install, clean install/uninstall, publisher-hosted URL and a clean multi-engine antivirus scan — a signature is not listed as a requirement. Steam charges US$100 per app and accepts non-game software only in a fixed list of categories, none of which is "utilities" or "desktop customization". Auto-update is solved for free by Store MSIX; outside the Store, Velopack is the documented cross-platform option but it must do the signing itself.

### Cited Findings
**winget**
- [Official, page updated 2026-07-14] Submissions are PRs to `microsoft/winget-pkgs`: "an automated process will validate your manifest file and check to make sure the package complies with the Windows Package Manager polices and is not known to be malicious." "Microsoft reserves the right to refuse a submission for any reason." "Once complete, your submission will be manually reviewed by a moderator". — [Submit your manifest to the repository](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/package-manager/package/repository)
- [Official, same page] Expectations: "The manifest complies with the schema requirements. All URLs in the manifest lead to safe websites. The installer and application are virus free. ... The application installs and uninstalls correctly for both administrators and non-administrators. The installer supports non-interactive modes. All manifest entries are accurate and not misleading. The installer comes directly from the publisher's website." — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/package-manager/package/repository)
- [Official, same page] Installer URL must be HTTPS (`Validation-HTTP-Error`), not a redirector (`Validation-Indirect-URL`), and from "the ISV's release location" (`Validation-Domain`). "Each submission to the Windows Package Manager Repository is run through several antivirus programs." If a vendor cannot clear a detection, "the application can't be added". Folder layout: `manifests/<letter>/<publisher>/<application>/<version>`; "We currently allow only one manifest file per submission." — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/package-manager/package/repository)
- [Official, same page] The label list includes `Manifest-Installer-Validation-Error` ("inconsistencies or values not present in the manifest during the evaluation of an MSIX package") and `Validation-MSIX-Dependency`, i.e. MSIX installers are an accepted installer type. The page contains no requirement that EXE/MSI installers be code-signed. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/package-manager/package/repository)
- [Official, same page] winget content policies mirror the Store's numbering (Policy-Test-2.1 … 2.12, including "2.12 User Generated Content"). — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/package-manager/package/repository)

**Steam**
- [Official, Steamworks docs, fetched 2026-11-16] Non-game software "may be accepted" only in these categories: Animation & Modeling; Audio/Video Production; Design & Illustration; Photo Editing; Educational & Tutorials; Finance & Accounting; Player Tools; SteamVR Tools (Drivers, Overlays, Utilities). — [Steamworks onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding)
- [Official, same page] Fee: "$100 USD (or equivalent) fee for each product you wish to distribute on Steam." Review of store page/build "takes between 1-5 days"; "A 21-day waiting period between when you paid the app fee and when you can release" (first titles); a public "coming soon" page "for at least two weeks"; tax verification "may take up to 30 days". Requires legal identity, matching bank account holder name, and a US tax questionnaire (W-8BEN-equivalent for non-US). — [Steamworks onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding), [Steam Direct fee](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee)

**Chocolatey / Scoop**
- [Secondary, Chocolatey community package pages] Each package version in the Chocolatey Community Repository goes through moderation: security, consistency and quality checking, installation testing, VirusTotal virus checking, and final sign-off by human moderators. — [Chocolatey Community Repository (example package page)](https://community.chocolatey.org/packages/resourcehacker)
- [Secondary] Scoop's "extras" bucket is for apps that do not fit the main bucket's criteria. — [ScoopInstaller/Extras mirror README](https://github.com/lyuha/extras)

**Auto-update**
- [Official, MSIX page updated 2026-08-24] MSIX via the Store: "Easier updates and better integration with Windows features"; the Store "will always use the highest-versioned package that is applicable to the customer's ... device". — [App package requirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/publish-your-app/msix/app-package-requirements)
- [Official, Store Policies 7.20 §10.2.9] For EXE/MSI Store listings the developer owns updates: "Whenever you have an updated binary to distribute, you must provide an updated versioned download URL in Partner Center associated with the updated binary." — [Microsoft Store Policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policies)
- [Secondary, vendor docs] Velopack: signing "needs to be performed by Velopack itself" because its Update and Setup binaries are signed at different points of packaging; its docs recommend Azure Trusted Signing (about US$10/month) as the most effective option; Velopack "supports building packages for Windows, OSX, and Linux". — [Velopack code signing docs](https://docs.velopack.io/packaging/signing), [Velopack Windows docs](https://docs.velopack.io/packaging/operating-systems/windows)

### Inferences
- winget is the cheapest non-Store channel for an open-source app: a GitHub Releases asset URL on the publisher's own repo satisfies "comes directly from the publisher's website" in practice for many existing manifests, and no signature is formally demanded — but the multi-engine AV scan is a real risk for an *unsigned* binary that embeds a script interpreter and makes network requests (heuristic false positives), and a single unresolved detection blocks the package.
- A Store MSIX app can also be installed through winget's `msstore` source, so a Store listing gives winget reach without a separate community manifest (background knowledge, not fetched).
- Steam is a poor fit on paper: a JSX desktop-widget tool is not in the published non-game category list. Wallpaper Engine is the obvious counter-example of a desktop-customization utility with a Workshop for user content, which suggests Valve's practice is broader than the list — but that is an observation, not a documented entitlement, and the US$100 fee is paid before review.
- GitHub Releases alone, with unsigned binaries, means every user hits SmartScreen "Windows protected your PC" on each new version and Smart App Control blocks execution (see section 4).

### Gaps
- The Windows Package Manager policies page itself (the numbered policy text) was not fetched; the "no signature requirement" statement is drawn from the absence of such a requirement on the submission page.
- No official Scoop or Chocolatey contribution rules were retrieved (only package pages and a mirror README); requirements such as Scoop's "portable/well-known/open-source" criteria and Chocolatey's packaging rules are unverified here.
- Steam's revenue share (commonly 30%) and its rules for apps that download/execute user scripts or use Steam Workshop were not fetched; Wallpaper Engine's categorisation on Steam was not verified from a source.
- MSIX App Installer (`.appinstaller` file) auto-update behaviour, its current status (the `ms-appinstaller:` protocol was disabled by default by Microsoft in late 2023) and WinSparkle (Sparkle-style appcast updater for Win32, needs its own signature scheme) were not sourced in this pass — treat anything about them as unverified.

## 6. Building and testing from an Apple Silicon Mac

### Takeaway
Windows 11 on Arm runs in Parallels Desktop (the only Microsoft-authorized VM solution on Apple silicon) with a normal Windows 11 Pro licence; nested virtualization is unavailable, so Windows Sandbox/WSL2/VBS do not work inside it. Release builds are better done on GitHub Actions: `windows-11-arm` runners are free for public repositories, and with a Store MSIX no signing secret is needed in CI. WinUI 3 / Windows App SDK apps cannot be built on macOS; Rust can be cross-compiled to the MSVC target with `cargo-xwin`, but MSIX packaging tools (MakeAppx/SignTool) are Windows SDK tools.

### Cited Findings
**VMs**
- [Official vendor page] Parallels describes itself as the only virtualization solution Microsoft has authorized for running Windows 11 Pro and Enterprise (Arm) on Apple silicon; supported chips now listed as M1–M5. A licence is required per Windows instance; Windows 11 Pro keys work for both x64 and Arm. — [Parallels: Microsoft-authorized solution](https://www.parallels.com/products/desktop/microsoft-authorized-solution-windows-11-arm/)
- [Official, Microsoft support article] Microsoft's guidance for Windows 11 on Apple silicon Macs: experiences that depend on nested virtualization are not supported (WSL, Windows Sandbox, virtualization-based security, Windows Subsystem for Android); DirectX 12 limitations are noted; Windows 365 Cloud PC is suggested as the alternative. — [Microsoft Support: Options for using Windows 11 with Mac computers with Apple M-series chips](https://support.microsoft.com/ja-jp/windows/-cd15fd62-9b34-4b78-b0bc-121baa3c568c)
- [Secondary] The authorization was announced in February 2023 for Parallels Desktop 18 on M1/M2. — [BetaNews 2023-02-17](https://betanews.com/2023/02/17/microsoft-gives-its-approval-for-running-windows-11-on-macs-with-m1-and-m2-chips-using-parallels/), [Petri](https://petri.com/parallels-desktop-mac-windows-11-official-support/)
- [Official vendor FAQ + Secondary] VMware Fusion (Pro) is free for personal, educational and commercial use (Broadcom made it free for everyone in November 2024) and supports Windows 11 Arm guests on Apple silicon; the free Fusion licence does not include any Windows licence. — [VMware desktop hypervisor FAQs](https://www.vmware.com/docs/desktop-hypervisor-faqs), [9to5Mac install guide](https://9to5mac.com/2024/08/24/how-to-install-windows-11-arm-apple-silicon-mac-free-vmware-fusion-13-pro-video)

**GitHub Actions**
- [Official, 2025-04-14] "Windows arm64 hosted runners now available in public preview" — free in public repositories, label `windows-11-arm`; the label does not work in private repositories. — [GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-04-14-windows-arm64-hosted-runners-now-available-in-public-preview/), [Windows Developer Blog](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2025/04/14/github-actions-now-supports-windows-on-arm-runners-for-all-public-repos/)
- [Official, 2026-08-20] "Windows 11 arm64 VS2026 image generally available"; the `windows-11-arm` image migrates to Visual Studio 2026 by default between 21 and 30 September 2026; a `windows-11-vs2026-arm` label exists for pinning. — [GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-08-20-windows-11-arm64-vs2026-image-generally-available)
- [Official] Standard GitHub-hosted runners are free and unlimited on public repositories. — [GitHub Docs: GitHub-hosted runners](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/runners/github-hosted-runners)
- [Official, sparse-package page 2026-10-03] Microsoft points to "MSIX and CI/CD Pipeline Overview" for building and signing packages in a pipeline with production certificates; packaging uses `MakeAppx.exe pack` and `SignTool.exe sign /fd SHA256` from the Windows SDK. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/desktop/modernize/grant-identity-to-nonpackaged-apps)
- [Official, 2026-08-17] Artifact Signing "integrates directly with CI/CD pipelines (GitHub Actions, Azure DevOps)" with no hardware token. — [SmartScreen reputation, Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/smartscreen-reputation)
- [Official, MSIX page] For Store submission the package does not need a CA-trusted signature at all ("The Store replaces any existing signature"). — [App package requirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/publish-your-app/msix/app-package-requirements)

**Cross-compiling**
- [Secondary, project docs] `cargo-xwin` cross-compiles Cargo projects to the Windows MSVC target from non-Windows hosts; pure-Rust projects need no prerequisites, C/C++ dependencies need clang (`brew install llvm` on macOS). — [cargo-xwin on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/cargo-xwin)
- [Forum + Secondary] WinUI 3 apps cannot be built on macOS (Microsoft Q&A answer on building Windows MAUI/WinUI targets on a Mac); Avalonia's comparison lists macOS and Linux as "Not a target" for WinUI 3. — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/1335698/should-it-be-possible-to-build-a-windows-maui-app), [Avalonia vs WinUI 3](https://avaloniaui.net/winui-compare)
- [Secondary, tool docs] electron-builder documents its Azure Trusted Signing integration as beta and running through Wine on macOS/Linux — evidence that signing Windows binaries from macOS is possible but second-class. — [electron-builder Windows code signing](https://www.electron.build/docs/features/code-signing/code-signing-win)

### Inferences
- A workable Mac-only workflow: develop/cross-compile the Rust (or other portable) core on macOS, run and debug in a Parallels or Fusion Windows 11 Arm VM, and produce the release MSIX (x64 + ARM64 bundle) on GitHub Actions Windows runners, uploading the unsigned/self-signed `.msixupload` to Partner Center where Microsoft re-signs it. No certificate or secret needs to live in CI for the Store path.
- Windows 11 Arm runs x64 binaries under emulation (Prism), so one Arm VM can smoke-test both architectures, but native x64 timing/behaviour and GPU paths should be verified on a real x64 machine or the x64 GitHub runner.
- The lack of nested virtualization in the VM matters for one specific tool: winget-pkgs' `SandboxTest.ps1` uses Windows Sandbox ([winget docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/package-manager/package/repository)), which will not run inside Parallels/Fusion on Apple silicon.
- Widgets Board testing requires "A Windows 11 device with the Widgets Board available" and developer mode ([widget provider walkthrough](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/widgets/implement-widget-provider-win32)); whether the Board is fully functional in an Arm VM signed in with a Microsoft account needs to be checked hands-on.
- If the UI were WinUI 3, the XAML compile and packaging steps would have to run on Windows (VM or CI); a framework-neutral native layer (Win32/Direct2D from Rust, or a cross-platform toolkit) keeps more of the build on macOS.

### Gaps
- UTM: no source retrieved on Windows 11 Arm support, performance (QEMU/Hypervisor.framework, no GPU acceleration comparable to Parallels) or licensing position; Microsoft's authorization statement names only Parallels.
- I did not open the `actions/runner-images` / partner-runner-images software lists, so the presence of the Windows SDK (MakeAppx, SignTool) and its arm64 binaries on `windows-11-arm`, and the current x64 labels (`windows-2025`, `windows-latest` mapping) are not confirmed here. A third-party guide states SignTool is preinstalled on GitHub-hosted Windows machines ([dev.to guide](https://dev.to/katz/how-to-set-up-code-signing-for-windows-apps-in-github-actions-cicd-pipelines-21ee)); verify with `where signtool` in a test job.
- .NET cross-compilation from macOS (`dotnet publish -r win-x64` / `win-arm64` works for plain console/library targets; Native AOT cross-OS compilation is not supported; `net*-windows` TFMs with WPF/WinForms/WinUI need Windows) is background knowledge that I did not source in this pass.
- Whether MSIX can be produced on macOS/Linux with the open-source `msix-packaging` (`makemsix`) tool, and whether Partner Center accepts such packages, was not researched.
- Current Parallels Desktop edition pricing and whether its built-in Windows download yields an activated or unactivated install were not checked.

### Takeaway

- [Official, 2026-10-08] Artifact Signing: "Individual developers must be located in the United States or Canada"; organization list excludes China; 15 service regions, none in China. — [Artifact Signing quickstart](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/artifact-signing/quickstart)

### Inferences
- Using the developer's own payment system for the paid upgrade (permitted for non-game PC apps under §10.8.1) sidesteps the Partner Center tax/payout profile entirely, which is where the 2026 China reports show breakage. A free app with an externally sold licence needs no payout profile at all.
- The realistic non-Store signing options for a China-based individual are (a) no signature (SmartScreen + Smart App Control friction), or (b) a traditional CA's Individual Validation / OV certificate on a cloud HSM. Store MSIX remains the only zero-cost, zero-warning path.
